The Enchanted Markets of Elyndra

Ambersorin Market

Where Even the Flowers Have Opinions

Seelie Autumn · Amberfall

Beneath the golden and crimson trees of Ambersorin, the marketplace sprawls across a bustling square, its striped awnings sheltering a wonderful jumble of extraordinary merchandise.

The air carries the aromas of roasted chestnuts, spiced cakes and sweet autumn fruits, while merchants call out their prices above the chatter of shoppers and the occasional indignant complaint from something that doesn’t wish to be sold.

The Stall of the Choosing Blooms

Amongst the market’s most celebrated attractions is a flower stall where the merchandise has very definite opinions about its prospective owners.

Huge orange blossoms turn their heads away from customers they dislike, while crimson flowers lean eagerly towards those they favour. Some blooms refuse to open unless properly complimented. Others have been known to follow a particularly appealing customer halfway across the square, much to their merchant’s annoyance.

The stallholder insists his flowers possess excellent taste.

Unfortunately, they rarely agree with his customers.

A Market of Mischief and Bargains

Elsewhere, jewellers display amber pendants containing swirling golden light, while tiny bottles hold captured autumn mist.

At the bookseller’s stall, beautifully bound volumes occasionally escape, scuttling across the cobbles while their unfortunate merchant gives chase.

Only the difficult ones, apparently.

The fabric merchants offer magnificent russet silks and richly embroidered velvets, although their opening prices can be rather ambitious. Bargaining is practically an art form in Ambersorin, and visitors are advised to bring both patience and a sharp tongue. By late afternoon, the square glows beneath the autumn sunlight, musicians gather amongst the stalls, and the scents of cinnamon and warm pastries drift through the crowds.

Visitors may leave with exquisite fabrics, enchanted jewellery or a flower that has decided to adopt them.

But in Ambersorin Market, the merchandise often has the final say.
