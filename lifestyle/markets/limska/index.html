The Enchanted Markets of Elyndra

Limska Market

Where Even Kings Must Queue

The Mortal Realm · Limskada

Beneath the grey-and-gold banners of House Caelithorne, Limska’s bustling market is a celebration of mortal craftsmanship, royal tradition and the enduring strength of Limskada. The capital’s pale stone buildings and silver towers rise above crowded market streets, while the magnificent royal castle overlooks the city.

Unlike the markets of the Seelie and Unseelie Courts, there are no enchanted bargains or magical curiosities competing for attention. Instead, stalls overflow with embroidered silks, polished silver, fine leatherwork, pottery, mountain cheeses, fresh fruit and fragrant spices.

Courtiers mingle with merchants, soldiers and townspeople. Traders arrive from distant settlements, bringing unusual goods from the forests, mountains and silver deserts of Limskada. The sound of bargaining fills the square, accompanied by laughter, music and the ringing of the castle bells.

The Famous Stall: The Honeycake Baker

Among the market’s oldest establishments stands The Honeycake Baker, famous throughout Limskada for its golden honey-and-spice cakes.

Made from a closely guarded family recipe, the cakes emerge from the ovens with crisp golden edges and a generous glaze of warm honey. Their irresistible fragrance draws customers from every corner of the market.

Even members of the royal household are said to send servants to purchase them, although the baker insists that no customer, however important, may jump the queue. There is no magic involved, simply generations of skill, excellent ingredients and a family determined to protect its secret.

And in a realm surrounded by the extraordinary powers of The Fae, perhaps that is Limska Market’s greatest charm. Here, mortal ingenuity, tradition and a little healthy competition are quite enough to keep the marketplace thriving.

Beyond the market square, the streets of Limska are busy with traders and travellers arriving from across Limskada. The royal court brings its own splendour to the capital, but the heart of the city belongs equally to the ordinary people who fill its shops, workshops and market stalls. Beneath the grey-and-gold banners, the bustle of daily life is a reminder that this mortal kingdom has its own riches, traditions and pride, without needing the enchantments of The Fae.
