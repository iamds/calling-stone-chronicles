Gracie's story began when I was 18! I wrote a short story including a mage, Temerin, a cat - Quinn - although he didn't have that name. And a stone - The Calling stone. But it really wasn't brilliant. Somewhere in the back of my head was the idea I'd improve it - one day! 

Then I became a teacher and for many years that's what I was, who I was. I loved teaching, I still do and I enjoy  working with young people. It's different now, but those teacher skills do come in useful. 

In 2018, I studied creative writing at a local college, adult education classes. Met some lovely people, some of whom I am still good friends with. I joined a writing group, I did courses. Lots of courses. My early writing was a learning curve. What I thought was OK, really wasn't. As a teacher/ director I was/ am a perfectionist, if things are not exactly what I want, I change them, over and over until whatever it is is perfect. Well, as close to perfect as I can get it. Sometimes you do have to say enough is enough. 

From 2018 to 2023 - I tried my hand at a variety of genres. Writing for children, not young adults, children. Then some crime, psychological thriller writing, short stories, and more latterly, writing for young adults and finally, romance. The idea of writing for young adults came from looking at the various things I'd written and realising that none of them really used what I knew. Schools, young people, exams, special needs and where my imagination could wander - into fantasy. 

They do say write about what you know. I hadn't, but then I did.

So the idea grew- a dual world existence, problems in school, dyslexia, a quest, magic, a talking cat. It was there, but a little muddled to say the least. 

I realised it needed planning. Mind maps, pages of notes, lists of chapters. Gradually an outline appeared and a story that I hoped would connect with young people and anyone who chose to read the books. 

The story of a sixteen - year old dyslexic student struggling with bullies, a home life that's far from perfect and exam anxiety. But that wasn't enough, I believed anyone could write about difficulties in school. I decided the Fantasy world, the mortal realm of Limskada, that was a place where Gracie could be tested, her strengths, her ability to see patterns where other people don't, relationships. Here she could use her dyslexia.  Everything echoes from her school problems but here, where magic exists, things are different. I didn't want magic to be the answer to all her problems, I wanted her to use her dyslexia to show readers it can be a strength. In both worlds, Gracie has allies, friends and people she has to learn to trust. That's not always easy. 

I hope Gracie's journey  encourages every reader to never give up - on themselves - because  through all five books, that's what Gracie has to believe in. Herself.
