The connection between Earth and Elyndra – World of the Fae. 

Many years ago, before magic disappeared from Earth, The Fae would visit and even live among mortals. But they were not always ‘good neighbours’. They delighted in playing tricks, causing disruption to mortal lives and creating chaos. Although some also took responsibility for the care of the environment, plants, flowers and weather. The characters seen in ‘A Midsummer Night’s Dream’, Puck, Oberon and Titania, are typical of the Fae of that time. In Romeo and Juliet, Mercutio’s Queen Mab speech is also fairly accurate. 

There came a time when mortals grew tired of being accosted by The Fae, attacked and pinched by their bony fingers and generally used as playthings. The Earth mortals asked for a meeting. 

During that meeting, unfortunately, The Fae refused to take things seriously. They laughed and danced, mocking the mortals for their lack of magic and refusing to listen to their demands. Consequently, relationships grew strained. 

What The Fae did not know was that the mortals had investigated what defence would work against the magical creatures.  They discovered that: 

·      Iron was deadly if touched by the fae. 

·      Salt could be used as a barrier.

·      Rowan wood – a defence against mind control.

·      Flint – would cause rapid decay. 

·      St john’s wort – was/ is a poison to any with Fae blood.

Further details are below.  

Weapons The Mortals used against The Fae

 

1. Iron (The Universal Disruptor)

* What it attacks: Their magical biology.
* The Mechanic: The heavy, forged nature of iron grounds out the fluid, supernatural energy that keeps a Fae's physical body stable. It burns them on contact, negates their immortality, and stops their wounds from regenerating.

. Salt (The Universal Boundary)

* What it attacks: Their illusion and essence.
* The Mechanic: Salt is a crystalline structure representing absolute purity and preservation. It strips away all Fae glamour on contact and acts as an impassable barrier. A Fae attempting to step over a line of salt suffers agonizing, chemical-like burns.

Rowantree / Mountain Ash (The Universal Mind Shield)

* What it attacks: Their psychological power.
* The Mechanic: While iron hurts their bodies, Rowan wood is the ultimate universal defence against Fae 

  *mind control*

  . The Fae are notorious for "beguiling," hypnotizing, or driving mortals mad with their voices, music, and presence. Rowan wood completely neutralizes these psychic frequencies.
* Mortal Use: Mortals wore Rowan amulets, carried Rowan shields, or planted Rowan trees at their gates. Holding it makes a mortal entirely immune to Fae illusions, charms, and mental manipulation. A Fae cannot use their magic to trick a mortal who is holding Rowan.

4. Flint (The Stone of Un-Making)

* What it attacks: Their primordial physical forms.
* The Mechanic: Deeply rooted in real Earth folklore, primitive flint arrowheads were historically called "elf-shot." Flint represents the raw, ancient crust of the Earth before magic or the Fae Courts ever existed. Because it represents a world of absolute, un-enchanted stone, it fundamentally rejects Fae biology.
* The Effect: While iron burns them, a puncture wound from a crude flint dagger or arrowhead causes a rapid, petrifying decay. The wound area hardens, cracks, and turns into brittle stone, slowly spreading through the Fae's body and "un-making" their immortal form.

St. John's Wort / The Sun-Herb (The Universal Poison)

* What it attacks: Their supernatural blood and life force.
* The Mechanic: In historical Earth mythology, St. John’s Wort was considered the ultimate herb for driving away evil spirits, demons, and fairies. It is a plant that blooms precisely during the Earth's Summer Solstice, absorbing the absolute purest, unfiltered mortal-world sunlight. Because it holds the essence of Earth's true sun, it is toxic to all Fae magic (even the Summer Court, whose magic comes from Elyndra's sun, not Earth's).
* The Effect: To the Fae, this plant acts like aconite or arsenic. Mortals would crush the leaves to coat their blades, mix it into oils, or burn it to create a toxic smoke. Contact with the juices of the herb blisters Fae skin, and entering their bloodstream completely paralyzes their magical channels, inducing a fatal, slow sickness.



The Complete Five-Fold Universal Arsenal:

1. Iron: Destroys their flesh and prevents them from healing.
2. Salt: Dissolves their illusions and creates impassable physical boundaries.
3. Rowan Wood: Immunizes the mortal mind against all Fae charms, songs, and mental manipulation.
4. Flint: Petrifles their bodies, turning their immortal flesh back into dead stone.
5. St. John's Wort: Acts as a deadly systemic poison that paralyzes their magic and blood.

 

In one particular exchange, between a Fae lord and mortal, whose name has long been forgotten, weapons were drawn. And even the Fae’s magic was not quick enough to stop the point of the iron tip of the mortal weapon piercing the Fae and killing him. 

The phrase, ‘One Blood, Closed doors,’ became associated with that event. 

The Fae withdrew all connection to Earth. Sealing all portals, policing The Passing Lands with careful guards, and banning the five substances known to harm their kind. 

 "One Blood, Closed Doors”.

The assassination of a Fae Lord by a mere mortal was a catastrophic event. It caused the entire Fae species to sever ties, slam the doors to Earth, and retreat into the isolation of their own world Elyndra—leaving the Passing Lands as a lonely, heavily policed buffer zone.

The mortals of Limskada, without magic, or links to Earth, were tolerated and in time accepted as part of Elyndra.

 

Generations and Seasons later, Limskada, because of its lack of magic, became a protected realm. Protected by the Seelie Seasonal courts. 

 

For the Unseelie, the realm of Limskada, The Mortal realm, as it was known,  remained a place of fascination, as did Earth.
