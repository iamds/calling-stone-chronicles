The Tenebrachnid

A massive spider servant of Smerdante, belonging to the Unseelie Court of Spring. It binds victims in living webbing and recognises the Gleamweaver.

The Tenebrachnid is a gigantic spider born of shadow and darkness. It lurks out of sight.Native to Ebonreach, it is a creature of Smerdante. It has many eyes and able to spin a thread so fine, it is almost beautiful in its ability to ensare.

When the company meet it in Ebonreach they have been warned by Gracie the way is dangerous. She is ignored. The creature traps the remainder of the group and chooses to speak to Gracie alone.

It gives her some disturbing news.

" There’s something on the stone.

Half-swallowed by vine and lichen, a series of etched linescurves across the arch.

Not words.

I dismount and step closer, brushing the ivy back. Loops.Spirals. The start of a lattice. My fingers tingle.

It’s not writing, not really. It’s…woven. A pattern. Awarning.

And it isn’t reading.It’s watching a whirlpool spin. Threads weaving. A dance of form and sense. Themeaning unravels and settles in my bones.

“Silken lair. Hunger coils and Hunger weaves. Beware thedream-spinner. Speak not. Stir not. Dream not.”

I blink, stepping back. The markings are already fading, likemist burned off by light. But the warning lingers. So does the tension buildingin my chest.

“Gracie?” Temerin calls. I ignore him.

I shudder. “We shouldn’t go this way.”

Temerin frowns, approaching. “What do you see?”

I point. “It’s… it’s a warning.”

He squints. “There are no letters. No runes even.”

“I know,” I snap. “But it’s shown.”

White cobwebs cover the wall in variousplaces. In the sunlight, it looks beautiful. The lace strands stretch acrossthe stones like a sparkling, silken veil, each one glinting with an eerie,spellbinding glow. The fibres pulse softly, as if alive. Tiny, glistening droplets hang like trapped stars. Itdraws the eye deeper. Attraction, invitation, temptation, as if onetouch would trap you forever in its silken embrace.

Suddenly, thedaylight’s golden hues drain to a pale, sickly light that casts warped shadowsacross the ground. Nothing stirs—not a breeze, not a bird, as if the worlditself is holding its breath under the command of …something unseen.

“Temerin what is this?” I point to the cobweb like covering.

His eyes go wide.

“Tenebrachnid!” he whispers the word in clear terror. “Dara, Tavian prepare yourselves.”

The princes don’t see what I see creeping through the haze. Legs—long, jagged, and impossibly black—emerge first. A glistening, bulbous mass that absorbs all light follows.Gleaming eyes catch mine, unblinking, cold, and its fangs, like curved daggers ,glint as it draws closer. It stops, waiting. The horses bolt.

That warning. Hunger coils.

My legs twitch ready to run, but I forcet hem still. One wrong move—one breath too loud—. I’ll be nothing but a ripple in its web.

The spider strikes, lightning-fast, spinning webbing so fine it rivals the air. The guards barely manage a shout before they’re tangled, a mass of sticky strands binding their arms and legs. Their swords clatter uselessly to the ground, muffled by the thickening cocoon that tightens with each desperate movement.

In seconds, another net spills from its spindly limbs, wider and thicker this time, catching the princes, Fallere and Temerin all in one brutal sweep. The more they struggle, the faster it tightens, ensnaring them in layers of glistening silk, shoulder to foot.

I’m alone. Quinn has escaped. My heart pounds as the monstrous spider pauses, its many eyes on me, as if savouring the moment. A chill races down my spine.

The spider’s voice is a low, rasping whisper, dry leaves scraping together in the dark.  Thick and echoing. Each syllable drips with a strange, cold weight.

“Cooome forrrward,” it hisses. It’s a summons and a command, hypnotic, laced with malice.

The spider’s eyes roam over me, each one glinting with dark, predatory hunger. Its gaze settles, probing, testing for weakness, searching for any hint of fear."
