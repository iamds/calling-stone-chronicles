Crime Across the Nine Realms

Although certain regions may acquire reputations for particular dangers, no realm possesses a monopoly on wrongdoing.

Although certain regions may acquire reputations for particular dangers, no realm possesses a monopoly on wrongdoing.

The Seelie Realms

Winter, Summer, Autumn and Spring are celebrated for their beauty, traditions and magnificent Courts. Yet beneath that splendour, ambition, jealousy, greed and the desire for power can flourish.

Criminal activity may be concealed behind legitimate businesses, influential families or apparently respectable political arrangements. The most dangerous threat in a Seelie city might not be the stranger lurking in an alley, but the charming individual who invites you into a splendid drawing room.

And the remote reaches of these realms can be every bit as hazardous as their darker counterparts.

The Unseelie Realms

Frostleven, the Mirelands, Ebonreach and Messavros possess their own traditions, communities and social structures. Some districts have acquired reputations for dangerous entertainments, ruthless bargains or criminal associations. Certain establishments may attract those who prefer to conduct their business away from official scrutiny.

But it would be a serious mistake to assume that every Unseelie inhabitant is involved in something unlawful.

Their cities are also home to ordinary families, honest traders, skilled craftspeople and individuals who simply wish to enjoy their lives. A demon may offer a stranger protection. A vampire may prove an honourable business partner. A werewolf might be the most trustworthy companion on a dangerous journey.

And, of course, any of them might also be a criminal.

Just as anyone else might.

The Mortal Realm of Limskada

Limskada is sometimes imagined as a quieter, simpler place, removed from the more extravagant magic and politics of the Fae Courts.

That does not make it free from crime. Theft, fraud, smuggling, robbery and disputes over property are as possible among mortals as anywhere else.

Its roads and rivers can attract bandits and dishonest traders, while its inhabitants may find themselves drawn into conflicts originating far beyond their own borders.

The absence of powerful magic does not mean the absence of ambition, resentment or greed.

Nor does it mean mortals are incapable of defending themselves.

Indeed, underestimating a mortal may prove a particularly costly mistake.
