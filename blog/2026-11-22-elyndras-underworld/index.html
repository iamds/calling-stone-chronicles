Elyndra's Underworld

Crime, Corruption and the Shadows Beneath the Courts.

Every realm has its secrets. Some are simply better at hiding them.

Elyndra is a world of extraordinary beauty. Its magnificent Courts, enchanted forests, glittering cities and bustling entertainment districts offer travellers countless opportunities for adventure, discovery and pleasure.

But beyond the music, laughter and splendid architecture lies another Elyndra.

An Elyndra of stolen treasures, dangerous bargains, organised criminal networks, political corruption and individuals who have discovered that there is considerable profit to be made from the misfortunes of others. Crime is not confined to the darker corners of the Unseelie realms. Nor does the splendour of a Seelie Court guarantee the honesty of those who inhabit it. Indeed, some of Elyndra's most dangerous individuals have never needed to draw a weapon.

And before we venture any further, one distinction is worth remembering.

A demon is not necessarily a monster. A vampire is not necessarily a villain. And a beautiful Fae is certainly not necessarily a friend.

In Elyndra, appearances can be wonderfully deceptive.

Welcome to the underworld.

Pirates: Trouble Upon the Waters

Elyndra's seas and waterways provide vital routes between settlements, trading ports and distant shores. They also offer considerable opportunities to those who prefer taking cargo to paying for it.

Piracy is an established danger in Elyndra, and its practitioners are not necessarily the romantic, swaggering adventurers celebrated in tavern songs.

Some are opportunists, attacking isolated vessels and stealing whatever they can carry. Others operate in organised crews, selecting valuable cargoes and maintaining contacts in ports where stolen goods can be sold. There are pirates who specialise in magical artefacts, others who pursue valuable commodities, and those who will seize an entire vessel if the reward is sufficient.

The dangers are not confined to the open sea. River pirates, dockside thieves and corrupt merchants can be just as troublesome. A ship might survive the voyage only for its cargo to disappear before reaching the intended buyer.

Nor should every suspicious vessel be assumed to belong to the Unseelie. Greed has never respected the boundaries between Light and Shadow.

A traveller's caution: A remarkably cheap passage aboard an unfamiliar vessel may prove considerably more expensive than anticipated.

Bandits: The Perils of the Open Road

Not every journey through Elyndra can be accomplished by magic, and not every traveller has the means or ability to avoid its roads, forests and mountain passes.

Bandits know this.

Some operate in small groups, targeting lone travellers and poorly guarded wagons. Others form larger companies capable of attacking merchant caravans and threatening entire stretches of road.

Their methods vary. A fallen tree may conceal an ambush. A seemingly injured traveller might be waiting for someone compassionate enough to stop. An abandoned tollhouse could have acquired new occupants who have appointed themselves collectors of rather excessive fees. There are even those who prefer deception to violence, presenting themselves as legitimate escorts before relieving their employers of their possessions.

And the respectable traveller should not assume that danger begins only upon entering an Unseelie realm. The northern reaches of Winter, the remote roads of the Seelie Courts and the quieter approaches to Limskada can all harbour threats.

The beauty of a landscape offers little protection against those who know how to exploit it.

A traveller's caution: When a stranger offers to guide you through a supposedly dangerous stretch of road, it may be worth discovering who made it dangerous in the first place.

The Black Market: Where Almost Anything Has a Price

Every society has laws governing ownership, trade and the use of certain substances or powers. And wherever restrictions exist, someone will discover a market for ignoring them.

Elyndra is no exception.

Its black markets deal in stolen jewellery, rare magical ingredients, illicit enchantments, forged documents and artefacts whose origins are best left unexamined.

Some traders operate from hidden rooms behind otherwise respectable businesses. Others conduct their transactions through intermediaries, arranging exchanges in taverns, private residences or secluded corners of busy marketplaces.

Not everything sold illegally is necessarily dangerous. A prohibited object may simply have crossed a border without the appropriate permission, or piece of paper, while a supposedly legitimate magical artefact could be stolen property.

But the more sinister trades are another matter.

Certain dealers seek powerful objects regardless of how they were obtained. Others supply materials intended for forbidden magical practices, asking few questions about the eventual purpose.

And some of their wealthiest customers may belong to the very circles that publicly condemn such dealings.

A merchant dressed in fine Seelie silks may be every bit as involved in illicit trade as a hooded stranger in an Unseelie alley.

A traveller's caution: If a seller insists that an object's history is unimportant, its history may be precisely what you ought to investigate.

Corruption: The Criminals Who Wear Fine Clothes

Perhaps the most unsettling aspect of Elyndra's underworld is that some of its participants are not hidden in forests, aboard pirate ships or behind locked tavern doors.

They occupy positions of influence.

Corruption can take many forms: bribery, blackmail, intimidation, forged agreements, manipulation of legal proceedings and the deliberate misuse of authority. A dishonest official might accept payment to overlook an offence. A wealthy noble could arrange for a rival's reputation to be destroyed. A merchant with powerful connections might secure privileges unavailable to competitors.

More troubling still are those who use the language of law, order and duty to disguise their pursuit of personal power.

Such individuals may never consider themselves criminals. They may even convince others that their actions are necessary for the protection of the realm.

Yet a law can be manipulated. An accusation can be fabricated. An innocent person can be punished while the guilty enjoy the protection of influential friends.

These dangers are particularly difficult to recognise because they often operate behind the reassuring appearance of respectability. The grander the surroundings, the easier it can be to overlook what happens behind closed doors.

A traveller's caution: A fine title, an impressive residence and a polished manner are evidence of status, not integrity.

Traffickers: The Cruellest Trade

Among the more disturbing criminal activities in Elyndra is the exploitation of living beings.

Its inhabitants possess an extraordinary variety of abilities, appearances and magical characteristics. To most, these differences are simply part of the richness of Elyndran life.

To certain criminals, however, they represent opportunities for profit.

Traffickers may capture rare magical creatures, trade in stolen eggs or young, or attempt to exploit individuals whose abilities are valuable to others. Some victims are sold through secretive networks. Others are coerced into service, threatened into obedience or held through magical restraints.

The distinction between creature and person becomes especially important here. A being may look unfamiliar, possess dangerous abilities or belong to a species that mortals have been taught to fear. None of these qualities makes that being undeserving of protection. Nor does an individual become property merely because another possesses sufficient power to imprison them. This is one of the darkest aspects of Elyndra's criminal world, and one that cannot be excused by custom, wealth or the supposed superiority of one species over another.

A traveller's caution: Be wary of anyone offering to sell a living being whose freedom, consent or origins cannot be established.

Assassins and Bounty Hunters: A Dangerous Distinction

There are those in Elyndra who make their living by finding people.

And others who are paid to ensure those people are never found again.

Assassins operate for payment, personal allegiance or political advantage. Their targets may be powerful nobles, troublesome witnesses, business rivals or individuals who have discovered something they were never intended to know. Not all assassins rely upon blades or poison. In a world where magic can conceal identities, alter appearances and influence perceptions, identifying the person responsible for an attack may be as difficult as preventing it.

Bounty hunters occupy more complicated territory. Some pursue individuals legitimately accused of serious offences. Others recover stolen property, locate missing persons or undertake dangerous assignments on behalf of lawful authorities.

But there are also those who accept payment without questioning the justice of their employer's demands. A wanted individual may be a murderer. Or someone who has offended a powerful person.

The distinction matters enormously. A bounty hunter who investigates the truth may serve justice. One who simply delivers a captive to the highest bidder may become little better than the criminals they claim to pursue.

A traveller's caution: Being wanted by someone powerful does not necessarily mean being guilty of anything.
